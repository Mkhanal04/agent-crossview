The screen budget

What one screen may show at once

The layout

One screen, one job

One sentence on top, one picture, and a panel for what you pick. Everything else is one click deeper.

Pictures

One picture per tab

One chart or drawing on a screen. A second chart moves into the panel or one click deeper.

Legends

About eight entries at most

The legend sits under the picture it explains. Smaller groups share one entry, like "Everything else".

Numbers

One headline number at most

The first sentence holds a number only when the number is the answer. Other numbers sit in rows, the panel or a hover.

Colors

One job per color

Orange marks what's worth your attention now. Blue is where something came from. The project colors stay clear of both.

Text

11 pixels or more

Everything on screen, including the words and numbers inside a drawing, at every width.

The check for a screen is the five-second read: someone who sees it for five seconds can say what it's for and the one thing it says. A screen over budget moves the extra part one click deeper.

Rules every screen keeps

  • One job per color. Orange marks what's worth your attention now. What ran in the week shown is the darkest grey. Blue says where something came from. Selection is an ink outline. Project colors stay clear of orange and blue.
  • Nothing moves when you click. Tabs, switches and boxes keep their size; details open over the page next to what you clicked. The checks measure every frame before and after each click.
  • Every number says how it was made. Point at it for what it counts, how sure it is and what it can't see.
  • Prices are pay-per-use estimates. They always sit beside what you actually paid.
  • Nothing changes on its own. A prepared change shows its exact lines, happens only when you press "Make this change", and has an Undo on the same card.
  • Plain words, in one voice. A calm colleague: serious, casual, respectful and matter-of-fact (docs/voice.md). One name for each thing, explained the first time it appears.
  • Contrast, repetition, alignment, proximity. What matters most stands out. The same part looks the same everywhere. Edges line up. Things that belong together sit together.
  • Text people can read. 11 pixels or more on screen, including inside drawings, nothing cut off, and text at least 4.5 to 1 against its background.
